Type 2 — The Giver / The Helper

Emotional center Social role: The Special Friend / The Confidant
Overview
 
Title and Identity: Type 2 is called the Giver, the Helper, or the One Who Helps. It is defined as the attentive, generous, demonstrative, and possessive type. This is a person centered on relationships, seeking to be loved and appreciated for their kindness.
Overall definition: Type 2, often called the Helper or the Giver, places love and helping at the center of their existence. This is a warm person with a natural capacity for perceiving the needs and qualities of others, in order to encourage them to fulfill themselves. They seek to be loved by making themselves indispensable through concrete help, often acting as a power behind the throne. The 2 is driven by the orientation toward love and helping. They possess a natural talent for perceiving others' needs, encouraging them, and acting as a warm confidant. They seek to create an image of an indispensable, selfless person.
Structure and psychological dynamics
 
Center of intelligence
Type 2 belongs to the emotional center, which it uses primarily outward.
Center hierarchy:
* Preferred Center: Emotional (outward use)
* Support Center: Instinctive
* Repressed Center: Mental
Orientation
Love and helping.
Compulsion
Avoiding the recognition of one's own needs.
Passion and Fixation
* Passion (Heart): Pride, expressed through the pride of helping without needing help oneself. Seeing oneself as the source of help without needing anything oneself.
* Fixation (Head): Flattery (to seduce) and Disdain (toward those who do not seem to need him or who are "selfish").
Defense mechanism
Repression (or suppression). He suppresses his own needs and the feelings that would not earn him others' approval, in order to project an image of absolute generosity. He unconsciously suppresses his own needs and feelings (such as aggressiveness) that could harm his image as a loving person, in order to gain others' approval.
Basic fears and desires
* Basic fear: Being unworthy of love, or not being wanted for himself.
* Basic desire: To feel loved.
* The Superego Message: "You are good, or you are all right, if you are loved by others and if you are close to them."
Origins and variants
 
The Childhood Pattern
As a child, the 2 often felt that love was not free and that it had to be earned by being helpful and putting others' needs before their own. They learned to become a "little parent" or a mediator in order to gain the nurturing figure's attention.
The Wing Subtypes
* 2w1 (The Servant): More serious, moralizing, and drawn toward thankless tasks or public service.
* 2w3 (The Host/Hostess): More extroverted, charming, ambitious, and concerned with their social image.
The three instinctive subtypes
* Self-preservation (Privilege): Feels entitled. This subtype sacrifices its own needs excessively but, in return, believes it has a right to special treatment and feels entitled. It expects special treatment in return for its extreme sacrifices.
* Social (Ambition): The ambitious one. Has a knack for spotting powerful or high-status people. Seeks to assist them in order to gain indirect influence and protection. Seeks influence and prestige by attaching to powerful people.
* Sexual (Aggressive seduction): A seducer who actively uses their charm to establish a strong, intimate relationship, often with people of power. Uses their charm intensely to merge with a target person.
Type 2 in the workplace
 
Professional profile
In the workplace, the 2 favors human relationships. They are positive, friendly, and have a vast internal network of relationships. However, they struggle to work alone and need their management's approval in order to be effective.
Management style
They are a mentoring manager who seeks to develop their team members' potential. They favor improvisation over planning and can sometimes "smother" their team with excessive help.
Communication style
Their communication is warm and modulated. They use the "Help / Advice" style, often standing close to the person they are speaking with in order to strengthen the bond.
Points of vigilance
They may neglect their own work priorities in order to help others, end up overloaded with tasks, or manipulate indirectly to gain recognition.
Arrow dynamics
 
Reaction to Stress (Disintegration)
Toward Type 8. The 2 then becomes abrupt, aggressive, hot-tempered, and demanding, openly blaming others for their lack of gratitude.
Path of Integration (Growth)
Toward Type 4. The 2 learns to connect with their own deep emotions, becomes more authentic, and allows themselves to be creative for their own sake.
Evolution and development
 
Points to work on
* Learn to set boundaries and to say "No."
* Only help when explicitly asked to.
* Separate rational factors from human factors when making decisions.
* Accept objective criticism without seeing it as personal rejection.
* Note their own daily needs and not help someone who has not explicitly asked for help.
Suggested Practices and Healing Attitudes
Practice relaxation. Tell yourself: "Maybe I could let someone else do this. Maybe I could do something good for myself too."
Integration (Higher Idea and Virtue)
By freeing themselves from their compulsion, Type 2 integrates toward Type 4 (or Type 8).
* Essence: This is the state of the personality freed from the automatisms of the ego, accessing its full potential.
* Higher idea: Freedom. They realize that their worth does not depend on their usefulness to others. Being freed from the need for approval.
* Virtue: Humility, which consists of recognizing one's limits and taking care of others while respecting their autonomy. Recognizing that love is not earned through effort.
Approach to the holy ideas (Spiritual perspective)
 
The Holy Idea
Holy Will / Holy Freedom. This is the perception that everything that happens is the functioning of the one reality, and that true freedom consists in surrendering to this flow.
The Specific Illusion
Separate Will. The conviction that there exists a separate "self" capable of making independent choices and manipulating reality at will.
The Specific Difficulty
Humiliating Castration. The feeling of being diminished and powerless when the environment's support is lacking, experienced as a wound to pride.
The Specific Reaction
Obstinate Action. A stubborn and prideful attempt to "do it one's own way" to compensate for the feeling of powerlessness.
The Core of the Fixation
The constellation between the feeling of castration (difficulty) and the activity of obstinacy/manipulation (reaction).
Energetic foundation
 
Basic Trust
An innate certainty that the universe is benevolent and that everything ultimately turns out to be beneficial without personal manipulation.
The Living Daylight
The quality of Being perceived as an ever-present Universal Love that continually sustains the soul.
The perspective and the solution
 
The Holy Idea as Psycho-catalyst
Understanding Holy Will dissolves the ego's cognitive error (the feeling of separation).
Restoring Basic Trust
This comes through recognizing that we do not have to "earn" our place or love, because we are already carried by reality.
Surrender
Ceasing to manipulate one's own states or those of others, and letting oneself be carried by the natural unfolding of life.
The Optimizing Force
Recognizing that reality spontaneously tends toward harmony once the ego stops interfering.
Analogy
 
Type 2 can be compared to a devoted host at a grand reception: they make sure every guest has everything they need, anticipate everyone's wishes, and seem to be everywhere at once to ensure everyone's comfort. Yet in their zeal to serve, they often forget to sit down at the table themselves, secretly waiting for someone to notice their empty plate without having to ask.
The Essence of the 2: Like a flow of natural warmth that needs no direction to be felt.
Type 2 is like a traveler on a train who keeps their heavy suitcase on their lap, afraid that if they set it down, the train will not be able to carry it; they must learn that the train (reality) is already carrying the whole weight, without their needing to make any effort.
Basic structure
 
THE type and its subtypes

General presentation of Type 2: The basic structure
* Wound: One day, while feeling a vital need for affection, the child had the sense that they would not receive it naturally, as if they had no personal worth. They give up their "true self" and realize that they can obtain a substitute for affection by detecting and satisfying others' needs.
* Passion: Pride. This is the pride of being of service to the point of denying one's own needs. The Two perceives themselves as a generous, fulfilled "giver," using giving as a strategy for seduction and self-worth. They have a massive need for love, often masked by an apparent independence.
* Preferred defense mechanism: Repression of needs. The Two excludes their own desires and feelings from awareness in order to focus entirely on those of the other. In doing so they avoid appearing defective (which might risk being abandoned) and often blame others in case of conflict so as not to tarnish their image as a helpful person.

Specific presentation items for each subtype
Choose a subtype
Self-Preservation Subtype
 
1. The SELF-PRESERVATION subtype
* Subtype title: The Self-Preservation Two Profile.
* Preoccupation equation: Two Preoccupation (detecting the other's needs and repressing one's own) + Self-Preservation Preoccupations (focus on home, security, and material matters) = PRIVILEGE.
* Behavior / Description of functioning: They devote their energy to the material well-being of their loved ones and, in return, claim privileges. They feel entitled to preferential treatment (being served first, having the best seat) because they make themselves indispensable to the household. At work, they often act as a power behind the throne, managing their superior's logistical needs with great empathy while expecting indispensable tokens of recognition.
* The paradox: Providing for the other's needs and receiving recognition does not prompt them to question their own real needs.
* Metaphor: The Nurse. Gifted at mothering, nurturing, and providing warmth (or the image of the Italian mamma who runs the household without seeming to).
* Type warning signs: Excessive worry about others, physical restlessness, excessive need for recognition, excessive self-sacrifice.
* Subtype warning signs: Excessive focus on security, too much time spent at work, invasive material concerns, routine evenings at home.
* Testimony (Pascale):
    * Type: Appreciates her broad intuition, able to sense what would do the other person good without necessarily acting on it right away.
    * Subtype: Eventually came to accept this territory as a reassuring "base camp" from which to explore other domains.
    * Combination: Found a balance between her active compassion and her spiritual outlook, feeling less obligated to immediately remedy every suffering.
* Film reference: Fried Green Tomatoes (for Evelyn's empathy, the tender bonds between generations, and the importance of food and clan).
One-to-One Subtype
 
2. The ONE-TO-ONE subtype
* Subtype title: The One-to-One Two Profile.
* Preoccupation equation: Two Preoccupation (detecting the other's needs and repressing one's own) + One-to-One Preoccupation (focus on the partner) = SEDUCTION / AGGRESSION.
* Behavior / Description of functioning: They use an intense presence and body language to win approval. There is pride in being the favorite or the confidant. If contact does not arise naturally, they may slip into an "aggressive seduction," pursuing the other in order to highlight them and adjust to their expectations. They seek emotional fusion and the enjoyment of the shared moment.
* The paradox: No success of their charm is ever enough to fulfill them, because their capacity for empathy prevents them from discovering their own needs.
* Metaphor: The Lover. Capable of anything to be loved, even moving mountains to intensify the relationship.
* Type warning signs: (Same as above) Excessive worry about others, physical restlessness, need for recognition, self-sacrifice.
* Subtype warning signs: Zeal to prove their passion, attention focused on the partner (pursuit), lack of panoramic vision, rivalry with the partner's other interests.
* Testimony (Pamela):
    * Type: Appreciates creating bonds, her positive attitude, and her ability to encourage others.
    * Subtype: Appreciates the speed of entering a relationship and reaching rare levels of intensity.
    * Combination: Succeeds in making others want to become "more beautiful" through the alloy of her encouragement and her aggressive seduction.
* Film reference: The Chorus (Gérard Jugnot embodies good-natured warmth, the need to protect children, and the creation of privileged, passionate bonds with them and with his music).
Social Subtype
 
3. The SOCIAL subtype
* Subtype title: The Social Two Profile.
* Preoccupation equation: Two Preoccupation (detecting the other's needs and repressing one's own) + Social Preoccupations (focus on friends, groups) = SOCIAL AMBITION.
* Behavior / Description of functioning: They seek self-esteem through social approval and the prestige of the groups they belong to. They are often the "ambitious by proxy" type (the chief's right hand, a celebrity's coach). They make themselves indispensable within an organization by being a mine of information about networks and power dynamics.
* The paradox: The more their social recognition grows, the less effort they make to focus on their own needs.
* Metaphor: The Ambassador. Jovial, creating "debts" through favors rendered, they put the success of their cause ahead of their own interest.
* Type warning signs: (Same as above) Excessive worry about others, physical restlessness, need for recognition, self-sacrifice.
* Subtype warning signs: Excessive concern with being recognized, too much time in social relationships, compulsive participation in clubs, excessive devotion to a cause.
* Testimony (Bernard):
    * Type: Appreciates his capacity to be of service, though aware that this compensates for a childhood wound.
    * Subtype: Appreciates the scope that social life gives him and his ambition for the well-being of humankind in general.
    * Combination: Feels effective defending community causes, finding a balance between his taste for independence and his inability to say no.
* Film reference: The Year of Living Dangerously (the photographer Billy Kwan knows everyone, handles strategic information, and lives vicariously by engineering his friends' success).
Definition + continuum
 
The 9 Levels of Development
The level of development is defined as a measure of the degree to which a person identifies with their personality structure, thereby serving as an indicator of their degree of freedom, awareness, and psychological health. At their simplest, they form a scale for assessing whether an individual is defensive and closed, or on the contrary, free and open.
The levels of development of Type 2 (the Helper) describe a continuum that measures the degree to which a person identifies with their personality structure. This continuum runs from freedom and unconditional love (healthy levels) to dependency and a sense of victimization (unhealthy levels).

Levels 1 to 9
 
The Healthy Range: Altruistic Love (Levels 1 to 3)
At these levels, Type 2 is able to take care of themselves while offering sincere support to others with no hidden expectations.
* Level 1: Self-care and unconditional love. The 2 gives up the belief that they have no right to attend to their own needs. They access their basic desire (to be loved) and feel unconditional love for themselves and for others. They become joyful, gracious, and humble.
* Level 2: Empathy and attentiveness. They focus on others' feelings with loving concern in order to protect themselves from their fear of not being loved. Their self-image is: "I am loving, caring, and selfless."
* Level 3: Generosity and support. They reinforce their self-image by concretely doing good things for others. They are encouraging, openly express their feelings, and share their talents with enthusiasm.
The Average Range: The Need to Be Loved (Levels 4 to 6)
This is where ego identification increases. The 2 begins to "give in order to receive" and to become more intrusive.
* Level 4: Eagerness to please. The 2 begins to fear that their efforts are not enough and that others do not want them. They try to win people over through flattery, excessive support, and trying to make themselves indispensable.
* Level 5: Possessiveness and intrusiveness. They worry that their loved ones love someone else more than them. To be needed, they put others' needs ahead of their own in order to have a "claim" on them. They become proud but demanding, never taking their eyes off others.
* Level 6: Self-importance and domination. They are angry at being taken for granted but cannot express their pain directly. They complain about their health, point out their good deeds, and remind others of everything they owe them. Repressed feelings cause physical problems.
The Unhealthy Range: Manipulation and Victimization (Levels 7 to 9)
At this stage, the 2 loses touch with reality, and their efforts to grow closer to others end up driving them away.
* Level 7: Self-justification and manipulation. They fear driving people away, which is often what happens. To save their self-image, they see others as "selfish ingrates." They use pity to create dependency and keep others from leaving.
* Level 8: Sense of entitlement and coercion. Desperate, they pursue affection obsessively. They feel entitled to get whatever they want because they have "suffered so much" and may act unpredictably or inappropriately.
* Level 9: Victimization and burden. They realize they may have been selfish or even harmed others. They collapse physically and emotionally, playing the role of martyr to force others to take care of them.
Warning signs
 
Warning Signs
* Wake-up Signal: "People-pleasing" (the compulsive tendency to want to please everyone).
* Alarm Signal: The fear that their efforts to grow closer to people are, in fact, only driving them away.
Growth challenges
 
Growth Challenges
Learning to nurture themselves, set clear limits, and dare to say "no" without guilt.

📋 Tendencies & Preoccupations
  • Seek approval and avoid rejection.
  • Proud of being needed, of being at the center of others' lives.
  • Express their pride in three areas: seduction (one-to-one), ambition (social), privilege (self-preservation).
  • Have difficulty recognizing their own needs and desires.
  • Repress their feelings in order to be loved.
  • Have boundary issues: "It's not that I'm taking charge of things, I'm just responding to the other person's need."
  • Strong in emotional empathy: adapt to others' desires as a way of gaining their affection.
  • A multiple personality: their self shifts depending on the people they are with.
  • Difficulty distinguishing their true feelings from those they perform in order to please.
  • Romantic attraction is an immediate source of energy. They live by and for feelings.
❓ Crucial Questions
  • Reference to others: Twos define themselves through others' eyes. They discover their identity in their relationships.
  • Are especially sensitive to the concerns of the person they love. Their attention is divided between others' feelings and their own.
  • A multiple personality: "Should I embody a gentle, aggressive, cheerful, or stern person?"
  • When pulled in different directions, Twos say they feel torn between their various possible options.
  • The feeling of being a selfless Helper conceals the need to be approved of and protected.
  • The demand for privileges arises when they help people succeed and experience the frustration of not being rewarded.
  • Exercising power indirectly, through others, feels more natural to them than saying what they want.
  • Twos take pride in their independence by believing that others depend on them.
  • If they are winners, so are you, and when their successes are celebrated, they expect to receive privileged treatment.
  • Pride conceals dependency on approval. Humility is the perception of one's true worth.
⇆ Security & Stress
  • Security (→ Four): freed from worry, Twos are no longer compelled to please at all costs.
  • Their attention turns toward themselves. "What do I want? What am I really interested in?"
  • A stable self develops. One cultivates one's talents and learns to value them with enthusiasm.
  • Difficulty in security: Twos may fall into low spirits upon realizing that it is easier for them to live for others than for themselves.
  • Possible self-pity: "Others had things so much easier than me. Why did I wait so long?"
  • Stress (→ Eight): Twos who resist it may become ruthless, as if fighting for their freedom.
  • The first signs of stress: they become increasingly preoccupied with the other's needs and repress their own.
  • Early signs of emotional hysteria: short bouts of irritability and emotional reactions over superficial matters.
  • If the source of stress does not stop, Twos will pull away from others instead of drawing closer.
  • When the movement toward Eight is liberating, it helps them recognize their own rights as a person.
❤ Love Life
  • Twos lavish the attention that others expect to receive. They will adapt themselves in order to please their partners.
  • These couples often give the appearance of "perfect love." Each supports the other professionally.
  • The most common source of conflict: the partner comes home late from work and falls asleep right away. Twos need a great deal of attention.
  • Both partners often discover their common ground at point Four: a mutual understanding of disappointment and melancholy.
  • Ones respect people who struggle, and Twos come to the aid of people who suffer.
  • The obligations governing the partner's life may be felt as repressive by Twos.
  • Threatened Helpers will fight to become the center of attention again. They will take pride in their partner reacting.
  • Twos in a couple must redefine their notion of relationship: half of a relationship is built by making oneself useful, the other half by developing oneself.
  • Twos in a couple are more often friends than lovers. As friends, they can mutually encourage each other to receive.
  • Couples of two Twos are rare. If one is generous by nature, one will instead look for someone to help.
📡 Signals
  • Positive signals: warm, empathetic, generous, good promoters, know how to build networks.
  • Are often indispensable figures in their field and essential allies.
  • Negative signals: indirect manipulation, a tendency to feel indispensable without actually being so.
  • May spread insinuations. When their pride is wounded, they dodge responsibility: "It wasn't my fault."
  • Their unstable mood can show through at work. A lack of respect, even implied, will irritate them.
💼 At Work
  • The power-behind-the-throne approach: it is easier for them to lead indirectly than to face hostility or rejection.
  • Playing the role of chamberlain, Twos observe, advise, and take the temperature of the situation.
  • Happy to cut down on administrative formalities and give priority to human relationships.
  • More productive in an interactive setting than in isolation. Often make their decisions together with trusted advisors.
  • Fulfilled Helpers are a treasure within a company: they represent you well, make constructive suggestions.
  • Their personal problems may seep into the professional setting.
  • When they first join a company, Twos need to be supported. It is at the start that they experience the most difficulty.
  • A team is cohesive when its members are of equal importance. But Twos often seek to attract the attention of someone in a high position.
  • Their tendency is reinforced if they can benefit from the goodwill of a well-placed colleague.
  • Twos are shaped by others' inner dreams. When they believe in a goal and are emotionally connected to it, they give the best of themselves.
