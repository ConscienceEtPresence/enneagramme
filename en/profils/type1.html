Type 1 — The Reformer / The Perfectionist

Instinctive center Social role: The Educator / The Teacher
Overview
 
Type 1, known as the Reformer or the Perfectionist, corresponds to a rational and idealistic personality, guided by solid principles, great self-mastery, and a high standard for itself and its environment. It is generally perceived as serious, upright, and deeply attached to notions of right and wrong. Its main driver is the desire to be morally just and beyond reproach. All its energy is directed toward upholding high ideals and applying constant personal rigor. Convinced that improvement is always possible, it continually evaluates its own actions and those of others against demanding standards, which comes with a strong sense of responsibility and sustained self-criticism. Driven by a deep desire for improvement and correction, Type 1 seeks to correct what it perceives as disorder, injustice, or imperfection in its environment, through concrete, structured, and ethical action. It has a natural talent for organization, teaching, and passing on values, and readily invests itself in defending causes it deems noble and just, often experiencing itself as carrying a life mission in service of a higher ideal.

Social role: The Educator or the Teacher.
Structure and psychological dynamics
 
Center of intelligence: Type 1 belongs to the Instinctive center, which it uses mainly inward, to act upon itself.
Hierarchy of centers:

Preferred center: Instinctive (inward use)
Support center: Emotional
Repressed center: Mental
Orientation: Personal rigor and high ideals.
Compulsion: Avoid anger at all costs (one's own and that of others).
Passion and Fixation:

Passion (Heart): Anger, which arises from the constant perception of imperfection in the world and in oneself. It is often experienced as a permanent resentment in the face of imperfection.
Fixation (Head): Perfectionism, which drives the person to work ever harder to reach an absolute that is often out of reach. Incessant checking and correcting of details.
Defense mechanism:
Reaction formation. This mechanism consists of transforming an unacceptable emotion (such as anger or the desire for pleasure) into its opposite (advice, help, or increased tension) in order to remain "perfect." It involves taking an unacceptable emotion (such as anger) and expressing it through its opposite (such as well-meaning advice or constant support), while repressing the original impulse.
Basic fears and desires:
➣ Basic fear: Being bad, corrupt, perverse, or defective.
➣ Basic desire: Being good, virtuous, balanced, and possessing integrity.
➣ Superego message: "You are good or right if you do what is proper."
Origins and variants
 
The Childhood Pattern:
The One often felt the need to justify their existence by being a "good child" and taking on responsibilities early. They experienced a disconnection from the protective figure (often the father), which pushed them to become their own moral guide and to rigorously self-discipline in order to gain their independence.

The Wing Subtypes

1w9 (The Idealist): More detached, calm, erudite, and often reserved. Seeks to improve things with a gentler, more philosophical touch.
1w2 (The Advocate): Warmer, other-oriented, persuasive, and active in social reform.
The Three Instinctual Subtypes

Self-preservation (Anxiety): Focused on self-control, health, budget, and domestic order. The person believes the slightest mistake can compromise their security. They obsessively strive to do everything right in order to avoid any danger. Often anxious about mistakes.
Social (Non-adaptability): The "Crusader" who represents objective values and seeks to reform society or their group. This subtype has a rigid view of how social interactions should unfold. They seek to correct and reform the group so that it follows the rules.
Sexual (Jealousy): Seeks a perfect relationship with an idealized partner and can become very critical if standards are not shared. The sexual One fears that their partner will reject them for someone more "beyond reproach." They are extremely attentive to the details of the relationship and can be very critical of the other so that they measure up to the ideal.
Type 1 in the workplace
 
Professional profile
Type 1 favors quality and ethics. It scrupulously respects procedures and discipline, viewing its work as a moral contract. It takes time to decide in order to avoid any mistake, favoring the concrete over abstractions.
Management style
It builds rational, precise projects. Once the plan is approved, it gives rigorous instructions and takes poorly to having them questioned. Its difficulty delegating often stems from its fear of others' mistakes.
Communication style
Its communication is often calm, precise, and informative, favoring advice over direct confrontation. However, an underlying anger can be perceived by others through physical tension or a curt tone.
Points of caution
Type 1 can get lost in details, which risks slowing down projects. Its rigidity and excessive criticism can frustrate its colleagues.
Arrow dynamics
 
Reaction to Stress (Disintegration)
Toward Type 4. The One then becomes melancholic, irrational, feels lonely and misunderstood, and can lose themselves in romantic fantasies to escape their burdens.
Path of Integration (Growth)
Toward Type 7. The One learns to relax, becomes more spontaneous, joyful, curious, and accepts a wider variety of possibilities without judging.
Growth and development
 
Areas for improvement to work on

Learning to be less perfectionistic by accepting that to err is human.
Focusing attention on the positive aspects of a situation before criticizing.
Practicing relaxation to release physical tension.
Being less demanding of others and valuing their abilities.
Developing a sense of humor.
Complimenting others and learning to listen to one's own emotional needs.
Suggested practices and healing attitudes
Telling oneself: "Maybe others are right. Maybe I've done everything that could be done."
Integration (Higher idea and Virtue)
By freeing itself from its compulsion, Type 1 integrates toward Type 7 (or Type 4).

Essence: This is the "true personality" freed from the automatisms of the ego, allowing connection to one's full potential.
Higher idea: Perfection. It realizes that perfection is the best compromise between the ideal and reality here and now.
Virtue: Patience, accepting the pace of things and the imperfection of the world with indulgence.
Approach to the Holy Ideas (Spiritual perspective)
 
The Holy Idea:
Holy Perfection. This is the perception that reality is a complete and just process in every moment. Understanding that reality is already perfect as it is.
The Specific Illusion:
Comparative Judgment. Believing that good and evil are absolutes and that certain things are intrinsically better than others at the level of Being.
The Specific Difficulty
The feeling of being "bad," a deep conviction of having an intrinsic manufacturing defect.
The Specific Reaction
Improving oneself. An incessant mental and practical activity to correct what is wrong.
The Core of the Fixation:
The constellation between the feeling of intrinsic defect (difficulty) and the obsessive activity of improvement (reaction).
Energetic foundation
 
Basic Trust
An innate, preverbal certainty that the universe is benevolent and that everything will ultimately turn out for the good.
Living Daylight
The quality of Being that resembles a loving, universal awareness, perceived as the very substance of everything that exists.
The perspective and the solution
 
The Holy Idea as Psycho-catalyst
It acts as a mirror that reveals and dissolves the ego's cognitive distortions.
Restoring Basic Trust
This comes through accepting one's existential powerlessness and discovering that reality supports us without any effort on our part.
Letting Go
Ceasing to manipulate one's inner experience and letting Being unfold without interference.
The Optimizing Force
Recognizing that the dynamism of reality naturally tends toward harmony once one stops trying to "fix" it.
Analogy
 
Type 1 can be compared to a rigorous sculptor who, instead of contemplating the finished work, sees only the tiny rough spots in the stone that must be polished without respite. Their inner "little judge" is a constant voice reminding them that the work is never truly finished as long as a single imperfection remains.
The gold analogy: A gold ring may be well or poorly shaped (relative perfection), but its deep nature remains pure gold (absolute/sacred perfection).
Basic structure
 
THE type and subtypes

type 1 "General overview of Type 1: The basic structure

Wound: One day, while spontaneously enjoying themselves, the child was reprimanded and suffered from it. They then give up their "true self" to adopt a correct, polite, and disciplined attitude, hoping to be loved in return.
Passion: Anger. This is an internalized anger and a resentment tied to a "stance taken against reality." Ones often do not express it directly but live with a constant irritation toward a world that should be more virtuous.
Preferred defense mechanism: Reaction formation. The One blocks their natural impulses or desires judged "bad" and redirects that energy into efforts to "do better" or take care of others (for example, transforming an impulse of anger into excessive attention to setting the table).

SELF-PRESERVATION Subtype
 
1. The SELF-PRESERVATION Subtype

Subtype title: The Self-Preservation One Profile.
Equation of preoccupations: One preoccupation (trying to do things right) + Self-preservation preoccupations (home, security, and materiality) = ANXIETY.
Behavior / Description of functioning: Meeting material needs (upkeep, education, meals) becomes so important that it generates anxiety. The self-preservation One multiplies checks, verifies details, and applies themselves to the task with discipline in order to avoid insecurity. They often feel they won't have time to do everything right.
The paradox: Becoming ever more focused on detail, and therefore ever more anxious, never actually manages to reassure them.
Metaphor: The Pioneer. The image of the pioneer from westerns, sitting upright on their horse, beyond reproach despite the dust, seeking to create a world of justice and tamed nature.
Type warning signs: Too much anxiety, too much bodily tension (jaw, back), lack of pleasure/laughter, too much self-sacrifice.
Subtype warning signs: Too much focus on security, too much time spent at work, too much preoccupation with material well-being, repetition of routine evenings at home.
Testimonial (Hervé):
Type: Appreciates their clear, immediate sense of situations and their "long-distance runner" endurance.
Subtype: Appreciates their sensitivity to atmospheres (warmth and light) and the vital renewal found in contact with nature.
Combination: Awareness of their anxiety focused on survival was the key element that helped them let go.
Film reference: Ben-Hur (for the discipline, the unimaginable effort through gritted teeth, and the determination to save one's tribe).
ONE-TO-ONE Subtype
 
2. The ONE-TO-ONE Subtype

Subtype title: The One-to-One One Profile.
Equation of preoccupations: One preoccupation (trying to do things right) + One-to-one preoccupation (focus on the partner) = ZEAL OR JEALOUSY.
Behavior / Description of functioning: They try to control the relationship with the other through an anxious zeal. Anger is often expressed through a veiled critical spirit ("you should") aimed at getting the partner to change. They may be jealous of the time the other gives themselves, or of rivals potentially "more perfect."
The paradox: Making every possible effort to be the perfect spouse risks stressing the other person and making them want to leave.
Metaphor: The Preacher. The one who preaches moral principles loudly and appears exemplary, all while feeling anger toward those who live more freely.
Type warning signs: (Same as the self-preservation subtype) Too much anxiety, physical tension, lack of release, excessive self-sacrifice.
Subtype warning signs: Zeal to prove their intensity, excessive attention focused on the partner, lack of panoramic vision, rivalry with the partner's other interests.
Testimonial (Aurore):
Type: Appreciates their steadiness, their honesty (incapacity to lie), and their inexhaustible energy.
Subtype: Appreciates the intensity of encounters and those "moments of grace" where a strong bond forms, even briefly.
Combination: Recognizes that her desire for intense moments makes her jealous of her husband's schedule, but she appreciates his faithfulness.
Film reference: Nelly and Monsieur Arnaud (for the complicity within a "bubble," the inquisitorial jealousy, and the remarks about how one should behave).
SOCIAL Subtype
 
3. The SOCIAL Subtype

Subtype title: The Social One Profile.
Equation of preoccupations: One preoccupation (trying to do things right) + Social preoccupations (focus on friends, groups) = SOCIAL NON-ADAPTABILITY.
Behavior / Description of functioning: They seek to reform the world, politics, or religion according to their ethical principles. They have an "aristocratic," superior self-concept, appearing civilized and well-mannered. They impose their standards with enthusiasm, becoming rigid toward those who do not respect social rules or ideals.
The paradox: Too much fervor for a cause can ultimately harm that very cause.
Metaphor: The Reformer. The one who knows the rules of the game perfectly, wants to improve them, and feels resentment toward those who take them lightly.
Type warning signs: (Same as above) Too much anxiety, marked physical tension, lack of pleasure, self-sacrifice.
Subtype warning signs: Excessive concern with being recognized, too much time spent in social relationships or clubs, excessive devotion to a cause.
Testimonial (Charles):
Type: Appreciates their inner strength available to others, their determination as a business leader, and their ability to bring structure to chaos.
Subtype: Appreciates their sense of the group and their respect for hierarchy and the skills of elders.
Combination: Leads with rectitude and integrity, trying to be beyond reproach in order to encourage others not to cheat.
Film reference: The Remains of the Day (the butler Stevens embodies conventional stiffness, absolute respect for the rules of propriety, and the sacrifice of emotions for the sake of work).`
Definition + continuum
 
The 9 Levels of Development
The level of development is defined as a measure of the degree to which a person identifies with their personality structure, thereby serving as an indicator of their degree of freedom, awareness, and psychological health. At their simplest, they form a scale for assessing whether an individual is defensive and closed, or, on the contrary, liberated and open.
The levels of development of Type 1 (the Reformer) describe a continuum ranging from spiritual liberation and ethical wisdom to an obsessive and potentially destructive rigidity. According to the sources, these levels measure the degree to which a person identifies with their personality structure: the further down the levels one goes, the more the ego takes control and the more freedom of choice diminishes.

Levels 1 to 9
 
The Healthy Range (Levels 1 to 3): Integrity and Acceptance
In this range, Type 1 is balanced and uses their ideals for the common good without being crushed by their own inner judge.

Level 1: Acceptance and wisdom. At this stage, the One abandons the conviction that they are in a position to judge reality. They become profoundly wise, discerning, and capable of accepting life as it is without emotional reaction, thereby attaining their basic desire: to have total integrity.
Level 2: Evaluation and reason. The person focuses on their superego's directives to guide and protect themselves from the "disorderly" parts of themselves. Their self-image is that of a sensible, moderate, and objective person.
Level 3: Integrity and responsibility. The One reinforces their self-image by living according to their conscience and reason. They are highly ethical, disciplined, and teach by example, setting aside personal desires for the good of all.
The Average Range (Levels 4 to 6): Fixation and Control
This is where most people operate. Identification with the ego increases, and interpersonal conflicts begin to appear.

Level 4: Obligation and effort. The One begins to fear that others are indifferent to their principles. They become serious, feel obliged to correct everything they see, and begin arguing with others to convince them of the rightness of their views.
Level 5: Self-control and order. The fear of being condemned for any deviation from their ideals grows. The One forces themselves to live permanently by their own standards, organizing themselves rigorously. They become punctual and methodical, but also tense and irritable.
Level 6: Judgment and criticism. They fear that others will ruin the order they have established and become angry about it. They react by blaming others for not living up to their standards. They become perfectionistic, opinionated, and often sarcastic.
The Unhealthy Range (Levels 7 to 9): Rigidity and Disintegration
These levels represent a profound disconnection from reality and a total loss of control over the ego's compulsions.

Level 7: Self-justification and inflexibility. The One fears their ideals are false, which makes them bitter and misanthropic. To save their self-image, they become extremely closed-minded, refuse any compromise, and become highly moralizing.
Level 8: Obsession and contradiction. Desperate to control their irrational desires, they become obsessed with the parts of themselves they wanted to tame. Paradoxically, they begin acting on their repressed desires while continuing to publicly condemn them.
Level 9: Condemnation and punishment. Realizing they have lost control and are committing the very acts they cannot tolerate in others, the One becomes punitive. They attempt to eliminate the "cause" of their obsessions in themselves or in others, which can lead to self-mutilation, murder, or suicide.
Warning signs
 
Warning Signs

Wake-up signal: An intense sense of personal obligation (believing everything rests on their shoulders and that no one else will do the job properly).
Alarm signal: The fear that their own ideals are actually false or counterproductive.
Growth challenges
 
Growth Challenges
Learning that perfection is a process and not a final state, and accepting that reality may differ from one's ideals.

📋 Tendencies & Preoccupations
  • Works by trying to attain perfection. Avoids mistakes.
  • Self-denial builds up anger. Unmet needs generate resentment.
  • Finds an outlet for anger by correcting mistakes and defending social causes.
  • Shows anger in three areas: jealousy (one-to-one), non-adaptability (social), anxiety (self-preservation).
  • Seeks to be ethically beyond reproach. Conscientious. Controls their own actions.
  • Think right! Moralizing thoughts block real feelings. 'I should, I must, I ought to have.'
  • Act right! Admires practical virtues: work, honesty, and effort.
  • Be right! Judges themselves severely when assessing their own actions: a merciless superego.
  • Feels guilty about not meeting the high moral standards they have set for themselves.
  • Has trouble recognizing the early warning signs of anger. 'No, I'm not angry, I just feel full of energy.'
  • Self-criticism turns into self-defense used to criticize others.
  • Leads by example. As a guardian of ethics, tries to enforce standards.
❓ Key questions
  • Justified anger: you know anger is about to erupt as soon as Ones stiffen physically and become overly polite.
  • When anger is triggered, it will be justified. Ones need to be able to explain their anger, which comes along with the memory of injustices suffered.
  • Forgiveness would feel like denying the wrongdoing. If one forgives, the previously committed mistake might happen again.
  • Mastery of emotions: Ones were not allowed to express their 'bad emotions.' As a result, they fear being unable to control themselves.
  • Repressed feelings take on greater importance. Self-discipline and emotional control have been elevated to the rank of virtues.
  • Compulsive tendencies: free time is a source of anguish. An overflowing amount of activity fills it instead.
  • The dilemma between what one wants and what is right makes decisions difficult. A wise choice should take every aspect into account.
  • Ones prefer not to be the only one voicing a criticism, for fear of saying something foolish. Their confidence is bolstered if someone else speaks up first.
  • Putting off the deadline: reworking the data 'just to be completely sure' does not feel like procrastination to the One.
  • Saintly Ones: some Ones, convinced they are acting correctly, have moved beyond self-criticism and compare themselves favorably to others.
⇆ Security & Stress
  • Security (→ Seven): usual preoccupations ease up. One relaxes more easily, slowing down in favor of leisure.
  • The shift to point Seven happens naturally when the environment changes. Ones are known to be radically different on vacation.
  • The negative side of point Seven: a desire to possess everything, anger stemming from years of deprivation. A narcissistic rebellion can be triggered.
  • Stress (→ Four): free time causes anguish, work becomes compulsive, pleasure is excluded. Self-criticism becomes more punishing.
  • If stress sets in, their attention turns to the preoccupations of Fours. They can become paralyzed by depression.
  • In a state of shock, they realize that good conduct and overwork guarantee neither success nor happiness.
  • The positive side of stress: the discovery that buried emotional needs must have a place in decision-making.
  • At point Four, what one 'should do' matters less than discovering what one would want to do.
  • Liberating questions at point Four: 'What are my true feelings? What work would inspire me? What kind of life would appeal to me?'
  • The developed One combines the Four's inclination toward being different, the Seven's tranquility, and the Perfectionist's rigor.
❤ Love life
  • This couple can strive hard to lead a perfect lifestyle. Projects cement this couple, but teamwork favors action over feelings.
  • Displays of tenderness can come after professional activities. Love shows itself as much through effort as through feelings.
  • A couple of Perfectionists accepts criticism. Criticizing the one you love is a way of investing in the relationship.
  • A conflict between two Ones develops slowly, shows itself through an explosive quarrel, continues through a silent truce, and ends with a gradual reconciliation.
  • A healthy quarrel does this couple good. As soon as the relationship allows anger to be expressed, the feeling of suffocation disappears.
  • Long-time One couples maintain that deep attachment depends on the ability to quarrel and that displays of anger are acts of faith.
  • Pleasure can cause anguish for Ones. Duties come before comfort. Free time is a source of worry.
  • A partner can help the One by taking the initiative for leisure activities or by organizing a more relaxed way of life.
  • When Ones get involved in outside activities, they will stop supporting the couple. Chores can become a source of problems.
  • Away from home, the One changes radically — like the kid who has finished their homework and can go out to play.
📡 Signals
  • Positive signals: conscientious, high ethics, hardworking, good sense of organization, constructive critic.
  • Have an intuition for how things could be at their best. Defenders of important causes.
  • Negative signals: rigidity, difficulty relaxing, a merciless superego, a tendency to find fault with others.
  • One-track thinking: right or wrong, black or white, no nuance. Can make others uncomfortable.
  • Their inner tension can look like positive energy in their own eyes, whereas others perceive it as anger.
💼 At work
  • Ones often seek each other out. It's a relief to find someone who understands the need to do things right.
  • Work well in a team when others are motivated and well trained. Love a job well done.
  • Easily spot details, flawed reasoning, and the weak points of a plan, but prefer not to be the only one voicing a criticism.
  • Adequate work is not enough; it must be absolutely beyond reproach. Can turn into an obstacle when perfectionism takes over.
  • If the team is weak, Ones may hold back their efforts. Emphasize skill development rather than competition.
  • Lead by example. Establish precise, long-term plans. Guardians of ethics.
  • Their participation will be affected if there is a strong-headed person in the team who pushes themselves forward.
  • Can have trouble making certain decisions, for fear of being wrong.
  • Their meticulous, time-consuming way of tackling problems can cause frustration in others.
  • Ones are at their best when their energy and output are put in service of people they respect.
