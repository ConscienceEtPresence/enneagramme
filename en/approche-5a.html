The 5A Approach

Awareness, Acceptance, Appreciation, Action, Adherence — a universal growth process for transforming the ego's automaticity into conscious presence.

The 5A form a method called the Universal Growth Process (UGP), built on five steps: Awareness, Acceptance, Appreciation, Action, Adherence. This method is used to transform the ego's automatic patterns into conscious presence. It doesn't aim to change your type, but to observe your mechanism, understand it, accept it, and then take a freer action.

Content alone isn't enough. Reading about the Enneagram doesn't automatically transform you. It takes a repeated, embodied practice, one that engages the body, the emotions, attention, and behavior. The 5A method exists precisely to move from intellectual understanding to a lived transformation.

Each type has a central belief, a deep fear, and an essential quality that recedes into the background. The 5A work makes it possible to spot the type's automatic belief, see how it triggers reactivity, and then gradually return to the type's essential quality: serenity for Type 1, humility for Type 2, veracity for Type 3, equanimity for Type 4, non-attachment for Type 5, courage for Type 6, sobriety for Type 7, innocence for Type 8, right action for Type 9.

The founding gesture

The Pause

A single deep breath is enough to disperse cortisol — the hormone of reactivity. Every conscious action is born from a tiny but decisive pause: breathe in (wait), breathe out (assess), then decide. Without this pause, we react; with it, we respond.

Breathe in — wait Breathe out — assess Decide — act

The 5 steps

1Awareness

Observe what's happening within you, without immediate judgment. Notice your thoughts, emotions, bodily sensations, tensions, fears, automatic reactions, inner scenarios. Become a witness to the mechanism instead of being possessed by it.

Question: What is happening within me, right now?

2Acceptance

Be honest about what is really there. Recognize: yes, I'm angry; yes, I'm afraid; yes, I'm seeking to be loved; yes, I want to control. This is neither approving nor resigning yourself — it's coming out of denial and shame.

Question: Can I acknowledge what's there without judging myself?

3Appreciation

See the value in what appears. Even a defensive mechanism once served a function: to protect, to survive, to obtain love, to keep safety, to avoid pain. Understand with gratitude what this mechanism tried to do for us.

Question: What is this mechanism trying to protect in me?

4Action — Conscious action

Do something new. Awareness without action isn't enough. Once the mechanism is seen, choose a more conscious response: speak differently, breathe, slow down, ask, set a limit, stay present, listen, repair, act instead of react.

Question: What truer, freer, more conscious action can I take right now?

5Adherence

Commitment over the long term. This isn't a one-time realization but a regular practice. A form of self-love: I commit to returning to myself, to practicing, to not abandoning my inner development.

Question: What will I concretely commit to, so as not to fall back into my old mechanism?

Two cross-cutting tools that run through the 5 steps

🔍Curiosity & Gentle Inquiry

Curiosity is one of our greatest resources: neutral, non-judging, like a childlike quality. It neither agrees nor disagrees with what it discovers — it is simply interested. It opens, where judgment closes.

Gentle Inquiry is its extension: a kind, agenda-free questioning that goes beneath the surface of reactivity. It lets vulnerability emerge — sometimes shame, pride, disappointment, or rationalization — without attacking them.

To ask yourself: “Am I being triggered by something from within (an old pattern), or by something external (a real present violation) that requires my attention?”

🧭The inner coach

Within each of us lies an inner coach: a kind, non-judging voice made of truth, encouragement, and loving discipline. It is this voice that guides us from automatic mode to conscious conduct.

The goal isn't to fight the type's central belief, but to learn to use it more consciously. How can I express my talents and abilities — those organized around my central belief — in the way they are designed to be offered to the world? This takes discipline: the discipline to practice, to stay curious, to start again after a setback.

To ask yourself: “Which side of my higher qualities am I embodying right now? Am I expressing them broadly and with dignity — or narrowly, with contempt? How can I return to my best self?”

Educational summary of the 5A by type

An overview to compare the 9 types at a glance.

What blocks Adherence

Long-term commitment is the most demanding step. Here is what most often sabotages the practice — so you can better recognize it when it happens.

The "visible" reasons

We're too busy. We burn out. We forget. Something more immediate or pleasant distracts us. We don't see results fast enough and lose interest. All these reasons are real — but they're often only the surface.

The parts of ourselves that resist

More deeply, a part of ourselves may oppose change because it fears what we will become, or the vulnerability this work exposes. These parts are old strategies put in place to protect us; they are not the enemy — they need to be understood and listened to.

Unclear intention

Without a clear intention, energy scatters. Before the practice: name the resistances, take stock of what this work can bring (less pain, more joy, better communication, more presence), and root the intention in that promise.

The absence of loving discipline

Self-mastery calls for a tender discipline: the discipline to try again after failure, to breathe your way into the pause, to return to the practice without condemning yourself. This is not harsh rigor — it is self-respect in action.

Adherence as an expression of free will

Adherence is the expression of our will — that inner force which directs our conscious choices and fuels our self-agency. Choosing to respond rather than react, choosing "for the greater good of oneself and others": this is the mark of self-mastery. Life itself then becomes our meditation: pause, centering, observation, gentle inquiry, and conscious conduct.

Final formula for putting it into practice

To use in a real situation:

  1. What is my type doing automatically right now?
  2. What am I really feeling in my body, my heart, and my head?
  3. Can I accept this without judging myself?
  4. What is this mechanism trying to protect?
  5. Which essential quality of my type is being forgotten right now?
  6. What conscious action can I take now?
  7. What concrete commitment will I renew tomorrow?
This is the heart of the method: no longer being only your type, but becoming able to observe it, welcome it, understand it, and move beyond it through more conscious action.
