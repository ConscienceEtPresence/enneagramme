The Three Subtypes

The Enneagram unfolds into 9 types, but each type is lived in three very different ways depending on the instinct that occupies us most in daily life. These are what we call the instinctual subtypes.

According to David Daniels, without knowledge of the subtypes, you have roughly a one-in-two chance of misidentifying your type. Here they are, simply put.

Survival

Self-preservation · sp

My attention goes first to my material security, my physical well-being, the comfort of my home.

homecomfortsecuritydetailsforesight

In daily life

  • A practical sense, good at handling material things
  • Creates a safe, cozy atmosphere
  • Anticipates, organizes, plans ahead
The trap: endless anxiety about “enough” — never enough money, security, or reserves.

Social

Group · so

My attention goes first to the group: my place, my connections, what's happening between people, shared causes.

friendsgroupstatusnetworkparticipation

In daily life

  • Understands the dynamics of organizations
  • Loves events, collective projects
  • Knows who's who, builds connections
The trap: confusing one's personal worth with the group's recognition.

One-to-One

Sexual · sx

My attention goes first to the intense relationship, the spark with one person, powerful moments, shared presence.

intensitypassionclosenesspresencespark

In daily life

  • Experiences time in intense moments
  • Excellent one-on-one, a natural charmer
  • Seeks deep closeness
The trap: needing constant intensity, to the point of confusing passion with fusion.

How to recognize your dominant instinct

1. Where does your attention and energy naturally go?

  • Survival · toward home, material security, my physical well-being, practical details
  • One-to-One · toward a special relationship, intensity with one person, the present moment
  • Social · toward events, friends, my place in the group, networks

2. Under stress, what do you instinctively do?

  • Survival · I withdraw home, I organize, I tidy up, I secure things
  • One-to-One · I seek the presence of someone close, I need intensity, contact
  • Social · I go out, I call people, I get involved, I surround myself with others

3. A perfect Saturday night looks more like…

  • Survival · at home, quiet, good food, a relaxing film
  • One-to-One · an intense one-on-one, deep conversation, focused presence
  • Social · surrounded by friends, a lively evening, varied conversations

4. Where are you instinctively gifted?

  • Survival · managing practical matters, physical well-being, practical organization
  • One-to-One · persuading one-on-one, creating intimacy, communicating emotion
  • Social · building a network, organizing the collective, reading group politics
A classic trap: people often confuse their dominant instinct with the one they reject. Someone who hates social events may be a repressed social, not a dominant survival type. Ask someone close to you — they often know better than you do.
Main sources: Beatrice Chestnut, The Complete Enneagram — 27 Paths to Greater Self-Knowledge · La Clé de l'Ennéagramme — Les Sous-types. The names of the three instincts are sometimes translated differently: Self-preservation (sp) / Social (so) / Sexual or One-to-One (sx).
A dominant instinct is not a label — it's a doorway to knowing yourself better.
